What Being a Division I Athlete Taught Me About Creative Work

By Jamie GoralskiNovember 6, 20264 min read

Before I worked in marketing and design, I played Division I soccer at the University of San Diego. My days revolved around classes, training, competition, and showing up prepared for a team that depended on me.

Today, my work involves digital signage, marketing campaigns, photography, and workflow automation. The setting has changed, but I still draw on many of the habits I developed as a student-athlete—especially when I need to solve a problem in the moment.

Make decisions while things are moving

On the field, you’re constantly taking in information: where your teammates are, how the other team is moving, and what space is available. You have to assess the situation and commit to a decision while the game continues around you.

Working in an event environment requires a similar approach. Details change, information arrives late, and materials sometimes need adjustments close to an event. I have to identify what matters most, understand the constraints, and find a practical way forward.

That might mean adjusting a layout to accommodate new content, finding another production option, or clarifying which information needs to be confirmed before a menu goes live. The challenge is making a timely decision while still protecting the quality and accuracy of the work.

Preparation makes it easier to adapt

Training gives you a foundation to draw from when something unexpected happens. You can’t predict every situation, but you can prepare yourself to respond.

I approach creative work the same way. Organized files, reusable templates, and clear processes help me move quickly when plans change. Knowing the tools and understanding the project’s purpose makes it easier to judge what can be adjusted.

That is part of why I’m interested in marketing operations. The systems behind the work can make a meaningful difference when someone needs an answer quickly.

Communicate when the pressure increases

In soccer, a quick, clear call can help a teammate make their next move. When the pace picks up, communication becomes even more valuable.

The same applies when solving a problem at work. I try to make the situation clear: what changed, what we need to decide, and what options are available. Sometimes the most useful thing I can do is ask one focused question before making an adjustment.

Working quickly also means recognizing when another person has the information or expertise needed to move the project forward.

Use feedback and adjust

Athletics taught me to receive feedback, make an adjustment, and try again. During a game, that adjustment often needs to happen immediately.

I carry that approach into creative reviews. If a layout isn’t communicating clearly or a direction isn’t meeting the project’s needs, I need to understand why and respond. Staying attached to my first idea can slow that process down.

Being willing to revise helps me find a stronger solution and keep the work moving.

Learn from the problem afterward

Once an immediate issue is resolved, there is an opportunity to understand what caused it. Could the request have been clearer? Was information missing? Would a template or an automated update have helped?

That reflection connects my creative work with my interest in workflow improvement. Solving something once is useful. Improving the process can make the next project easier for everyone involved.

My athletic background continues to shape how I work: stay aware, make thoughtful decisions, communicate clearly, and adjust as new information comes in. Those habits help me contribute when there is time to plan—and when a solution is needed in the moment.
