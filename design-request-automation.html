The challenge

Get the brief out
of the email thread.

Requests arrived with missing details or files, leaving the team to chase information before starting production. Requesters also needed to know that their submission had reached us.

My contribution

Connect intake
and communication.

I created a form for the brief, deadline, contact details, and supporting files, with each submission recorded in SharePoint. I connected request data to Power Automate notifications and designed branded HTML emails for the team and requester.

The workflow

One request. A shared record.

The tracker holds the project details while the notifications make the next action clear. The team reviews and assigns the work; the automation supports that handoff.

  1. 01 · IntakeCollect the brief

    The form captures the project, requester, deadline, and files.

  2. 02 · TrackCreate a SharePoint record

    Keep the request and its supporting information together.

  3. 03 · NotifySend the team an email

    Power Automate brings the brief and due date into the inbox.

From a new record to a notification

The intake flow starts when a SharePoint item is created, retrieves its attachment information, and sends the Design Shop an email. The email directs the team to the request for its files.

Assignment remains a team decision. A separate acknowledgment confirms receipt to the requester.

Email design

Useful information.
A clear next step.

I developed branded HTML templates using SharePoint request data for intake, review notifications, and completion notices. Each message connects the request’s current stage with what the recipient needs to do next.

Request received

Confirm receipt to the requester and give the team the brief and deadline.

Ready for review

Prompt the requester to review the work and provide feedback.

Request completed

Communicate completion and the relevant next steps for the materials.

The outcome

A clearer handoff from request to delivery.

The process gives the team a central brief, deadline, and supporting files, while keeping requesters informed through email. It brings my work in content organization, HTML email design, and marketing operations into one practical system.
